BMW Timeline

1916 - BMW is born.

BMW’s origins lie in two aviation-related companies: the aircraft manufacturer Flugmaschinenfabrik Gustav Otto and the aero engine producer Rapp Motorenwerke.
In 1916, at the request of the government, Flugmaschinenfabrik Gustav Otto was reorganised and renamed the Bayerische Flugzeugwerke (Bavarian Aircraft Factory, BFW).
One year later, Rapp Motorenwerke was also renamed, becoming Bayerische Motorenwerke GmbH (Bavarian Motor Works, BMW).
In 1918, BMW became a public limited company – BMW AG – which in 1922 transferred its name, assets and engine operations to BFW. Since then, the foundation of BFW on 7 March 1916 has marked the official founding date of BMW.

1917 - The Logo.

Since 1917, every BMW product has carried the iconic blue-and-white logo, inspired by the colours of Bavaria. The emblem has become a symbol of BMW’s heritage, precision, and engineering excellence.
The logo was first portrayed as a stylised rotating propeller in BMW advertising materials in the late 1920s, reflecting the company’s early history in aircraft-engine manufacturing. Although the propeller interpretation originated in advertising, it became strongly associated with the BMW brand and remains an enduring part of its identity to this day.

1922 - New home, new beginning.

After the end of World War I, when aero engine production was no longer permitted, BMW switched to manufacturing railway brakes and inboard engines.
In 1920 the company was sold to Knorr-Bremse AG – but the financier Camillo Castiglioni purchased the BMW name and logo, along with the engine production facilities and workforce, and integrated them into BFW. In the same year, the company relocated to the BFW plant in Munich’s Oberwiesenfeld district – which remains home to BMW’s main plant and headquarters to this day.

1923 – BMW R 32: BMW's first motorcycle.

The Berlin Motor Show of 1923 marked the beginning of BMW’s motorcycle success story, when the aero engine manufacturer unveiled its first bike, the BMW R 32.
Quality played a decisive role in the model’s success: the components most susceptible to failure were enclosed, and the drive shaft was easier to maintain than standard chains or belts. The Boxer engine with its transversely mounted cylinders and the drive shaft remain characteristic features of BMW bikes to this day. The overall design – engineered by Max Friz – proved highly successful, and the R 32 became a milestone in motorcycle history.

1928 – BMW enters car production.

BMW first entered the world of automobile production when it took over the vehicle manufacturer Fahrzeugfabrik Eisenach in the state of Thuringia.
From 1929 until the outbreak of the Second World War, all BMW cars were built at the Eisenach plant, starting with small models made under licence from the Austin Motor Company and culminating in BMW’s own models in 1932.

1945 – A difficult return.

After World War II, BMW faced a difficult period as Allied troops requisitioned and occupied its plants, dismantling much of the machinery and equipment due to the company’s involvement in armaments production. With automobile and aircraft production halted, BMW had to find new ways to survive.
From 1945, the BMW plants in Milbertshofen and Berlin temporarily shifted their focus to producing household appliances and other everyday goods. This marked a challenging but important period in BMW’s history as the company worked to rebuild its operations and find a path toward its future.

1951 – BMW 501 becomes BMW's first post-war car.

In 1952, BMW introduced the BMW 501, its first new car of the post-war era. Built from 1952 onwards, the luxury sedan was powered by an improved version of the pre-war six-cylinder engine used in the BMW 326 and could accommodate up to six passengers.
Although the BMW 501 achieved only limited commercial success, it played an important role in rebuilding the company’s reputation. With its elegant design, advanced engineering, and focus on quality, the 501 helped re-establish BMW as a manufacturer of premium automobiles.

1961 – Breakthrough with the Neue Klasse.

At the Frankfurt Motor Show, BMW unveiled the BMW 1500, a model designed to fill an important gap in the automotive market. With its compact proportions, sporty character, and modern four-door design, the 1500 immediately attracted widespread attention.
The model became a major turning point for BMW, finally establishing the company as a successful and contemporary carmaker. Demand was so strong that orders for the BMW 1500 quickly exceeded BMW’s production capacity, marking the beginning of a new and successful era for the brand.

1967 – New facilities in Dingolfing.

By the mid-1960s, BMW’s Munich plant was reaching the limits of its production capacity, creating the need for a larger manufacturing facility. During this period, Hans Glas GmbH, based in Dingolfing, faced a serious financial crisis, giving BMW the opportunity to take over and restructure its facilities in Dingolfing and Landshut.
Over the following decades, the Dingolfing plant grew into a key part of BMW’s global production network. It eventually became BMW’s largest production facility worldwide, a position it held for many years.

1972 – BMW Motorsport GmbH is founded.

BMW consolidated its growing motorsport activities into a single company, BMW Motorsport GmbH, bringing together its racing expertise and engineering capabilities. The organisation quickly achieved numerous successes across motorsport, strengthening BMW’s reputation for performance and innovation.
BMW Motorsport GmbH also became involved in developing and producing high-performance road cars, laying the foundation for what would later become BMW M GmbH. Its influence helped establish the motorsport-inspired character that continues to define BMW M models today.

1973 – BMW builds iconic HQ and BMW Museum.

Construction of BMW’s new administrative headquarters in northern Munich began in 1970. With its distinctive four-cylinder design, the building quickly became an iconic landmark for both BMW and the city itself.
Alongside the headquarters, BMW also built the BMW Museum, featuring its unique bowl-shaped architecture. Together, the new buildings formed an impressive symbol of BMW’s growing presence and modern identity. The entire complex was officially opened on 18 May 1973.

1990 – BMW Research and Innovation Centre.

In 1986 BMW AG became the first carmaker to centralise all its R&D under a single roof. The Research and Innovation Centre – known by employees as the FIZ – accommodated an integrated team of around 7,000 scientists, engineers, designers, managers and technicians.
It officially opened on 27 April 1990. In 2004 the complex was expanded, with the addition of the Projekthaus. This was planned according to the principles of “constructed communication”. It was built in the space of two years and has a footprint of 12,000 m2. The nine-story building has an open gallery-style layout with an atrium. Its offices, studios and meeting rooms form the new heart of the FIZ.

1998 – Rolls Royce.

In July 1998, BMW acquired a piece of automotive history. Following long negotiations, the company obtains the brand and naming rights for Rolls-Royce motor cars from Rolls-Royce plc. Rolls-Royce is held entirely by Volkswagen until the end of 2002, when BMW takes on full responsibility for Rolls-Royce Motor Cars, along with all rights.
The new Rolls-Royce plant and a new company headquarters are built in Goodwood, in southern England. This is the sixth facility constructed since 1904, scheduled to manufacture newly developed Rolls-Royce models from the start of 2003.

2001 – MINI. Premium in the small-car segment.

First unveiled as a concept car in 1997, the new MINI was launched by the BMW Group in 2001 as the first premium vehicle in the small-car segment. Its innovative design, compact proportions, and distinctive athletic character quickly made it an icon of modern urban mobility.
Combining playful styling with engaging driving dynamics, the MINI captured the lifestyle and personality of an entire generation. The MINI Hatch was the first model introduced in 2001, followed by a growing range of variants that expanded the brand’s presence around the world.

2007 – BMW Welt opens.

Situated west of BMW’s iconic “four-cylinder” headquarters, BMW Welt was opened in October as a modern gateway to the BMW brand. Designed by the renowned Vienna-based architectural firm Coop Himmelb(l)au, the striking structure also serves as a delivery centre where customers can experience the excitement of receiving their new BMW.
Together with the BMW Museum and the plant tour, BMW Welt creates an immersive experience that brings the brand’s history, present, and future together. Its innovative architecture and interactive spaces showcase BMW’s commitment to design, technology, and mobility.

2013 – BMW i3 is launched.

The BMW i3 marked a new chapter for the BMW Group, bringing electric mobility into a new era. Introduced under the BMW i sub-brand, it became the company’s first series-produced fully electric model, combining innovative technology with a distinctive, futuristic design.
With its lightweight construction, responsive handling, and agile driving dynamics, the i3 delivered a completely new kind of driving experience. More than just an electric car, it represented BMW’s vision of sustainable mobility without compromising on the joy of driving.

2016 – Centenary and the Next 100 years.

In 2016, the BMW Group celebrated its centenary under the inspiring motto “The Next 100 Years.” While honoring a century of innovation and achievements, the celebrations focused strongly on the future and BMW’s vision for the next generation of mobility.
To mark this milestone, BMW presented four visionary concept vehicles, representing each of its four brands: BMW, MINI, Rolls-Royce, and BMW Motorrad. Together, the Vision Next 100 concepts showcased how design, technology, sustainability, and intelligent mobility could shape the next century of the BMW Group.

2022 – Presenting the Neue Klasse.

In 2022, the BMW Group announced that production of the Neue Klasse would begin in 2025, marking a major step toward the company’s future. More than just a new model series, the Neue Klasse represents BMW’s vision for a new generation of technology, design, and electric mobility.
At its core is a completely new all-electric drive system, designed to deliver greater efficiency and increased range. Combined with BMW’s newly developed generation of battery cells and other technological innovations, the Neue Klasse is set to accelerate the company’s transition toward a more electric and sustainable future.

2025 – Debut of the first Neue Klasse model.

In September 2025 the BMW Group heralded a new chapter in its history with the debut of the new BMW iX3 at the IAA Mobility in Munich. As the first production model of the Neue Klasse, the iX3 brings a new era of Sheer Driving Pleasure to the road.
The fully electric BMW iX3 50 xDrive combines sixth-generation BMW eDrive technology with a range of 805 km (WLTP) and peak charging capacity of 400 kW. As part of the new electronics architecture, the central control unit – called the Heart of Joy – comprises four high-performance computers, known as Superbrains. Together with the intuitive BMW Panoramic iDrive, it stands for the modular innovation toolkit that will form the basis for 40 new models and updates by 2027.
